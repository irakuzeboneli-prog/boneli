From tension to understanding

Peace takes people working together.

There is no single fix for every conflict. But inclusive dialogue, fair institutions, protection for civilians, and long-term investment in trust can all help prevent violence and support recovery.

Solutions grow when more people are heard.
A path forward

Different tools. One shared goal: safety with dignity.

Peacebuilding works best when it responds to the causes and needs of a particular community—and when the people most affected help shape the way forward.

01 / Talk

Open inclusive dialogue

Bring people together to speak honestly, listen, identify shared needs, and make sure different groups can take part in decisions.

02 / Resolve

Use mediation and negotiation

Trusted facilitators can help people address disagreements, explore options, and reach agreements without resorting to violence.

03 / Protect

Keep people safe

Protecting civilians, upholding humanitarian principles, and making access to essential support possible are urgent priorities during conflict.

04 / Repair

Support justice and accountability

Fair processes that address harm and protect rights can help rebuild confidence and reduce the risk of future retaliation.

05 / Rebuild

Restore services and livelihoods

Reopening schools, supporting health and community services, and restoring ways to earn a living help people plan for a future.

06 / Sustain

Build trust over time

Peace agreements need continued care: follow-through, inclusive leadership, community networks, and space to address new tensions.

It starts nearby, too

Everyday peacebuilding matters.

Communities, schools, and families can make it easier to resolve disagreements before they grow: create respectful spaces for discussion, challenge dehumanizing language, and include people who are often left out.

  • Listen carefully before responding.
  • Look for reliable information and context.
  • Support local efforts that help people connect.
