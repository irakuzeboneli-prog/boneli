Understand the roots

How does conflict take hold?

Conflict can grow when grievances go unheard, people are excluded from decisions, or fear and mistrust take root. Understanding the conditions that contribute to violence can help communities address them earlier.

Every conflict has its own story.
There is rarely one cause

Pressures can build—and reinforce one another.

These factors do not make violence inevitable. The details differ from place to place, and the same issue can be addressed peacefully when people have fair ways to resolve it.

01 / Unequal opportunity

Exclusion and unfairness

When groups are shut out of public decisions or face persistent barriers to rights and resources, frustration and distrust may deepen.

02 / Pressure

Competition over resources

Disputes over land, water, livelihoods, or other scarce resources can intensify when needs are unmet and solutions feel out of reach.

03 / Unheard grievances

Weak paths to justice

When people cannot safely raise concerns, seek accountability, or trust institutions to act fairly, unresolved harms can compound.

04 / Fear and mistrust

Polarization and misinformation

Rumors and dehumanizing language can spread fear, harden divisions, and make it more difficult for people to see one another's humanity.

05 / Past harms

Grievances passed on

Unacknowledged violence and historical injustice can shape how communities understand the present and whether they trust future promises.

06 / Escalation

Cycles of retaliation

Fear and harm can fuel retaliation. Without credible ways to de-escalate, protect people, and resolve disputes, tensions may spiral.

Keep the context in view

Differences alone don't cause wars.

People with different identities, beliefs, or backgrounds live together peacefully around the world. Conflict is shaped by choices, institutions, histories, and circumstances—not by difference itself.

Listening to affected communities and learning the local history helps avoid easy explanations and harmful stereotypes.
